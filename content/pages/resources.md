You don't need to be an engineer. A flashlight, a camera, and a short letter are enough to ask for warmer, downward light on your own block.

## 1. Audit your own street

- **Look up.** Can you see the Milky Way? If not, what's glowing — a specific fixture, a parking lot, a ballpark?
- **Look sideways.** Find every light where you can see the bare bulb from across the street. A shade or a new head will make that glow useful again.
- **Look at your bedroom at midnight.** Is any light spilling in? Note the source and the time.
- **Note the color.** Warm amber, or cool blue-white? Warmer light stays closer to the ground and to sleep.

Take photos and write down the make/model if you can see it. A specific note helps more than a general one.

## 3. The one-page standard to ask for

- **≤3000K** color temperature for all public outdoor lighting.
- **Full shielding** — zero uplight, flat lens.
- **Dim to ≤50%** from midnight to 5 a.m.
- **Lumen caps** tied to road classification, not vendor default.

This is, nearly word for word, the guidance of the **International Dark-Sky Association** — and dozens of municipalities (Flagstaff, AZ; the island of Sark; and many others) have adopted versions of it without losing safety.

## 4. Model ordinances & allies

- **International Dark-Sky Association (IDA)** — `darksky.org` — the central resource: model lighting ordinances, fixture seal of approval, and a growing list of certified Dark Sky Places.
- **IDA's "Model Lighting Ordinance"** — a ready-to-adapt legal template you can hand to your city attorney.
- **Your local astronomy club** — they've cared about this for a long time, and someone will often come with you to a meeting.

## 5. Lead with what towns already love

Safer streets, quieter bills, fewer late-night calls from bedrooms that never go dark. Start there. The stars come along.

## 6. Turn your iPhone all the way red (triple-click)

Your phone is often the brightest white light at a dark site. iOS already has a hidden red screen — **Color Tint** — and a **triple-click** to flip it on and off. Astronomers have used this for years. Set it once.

**Make the screen red**

1. Open **Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Color Filters**.
2. Turn **Color Filters** on.
3. Tap **Color Tint** (not Grayscale or the color-blind presets).
4. Drag **Intensity** all the way right, then **Hue** all the way right. The whole display goes deep red.

**Make it a three-click toggle**

1. Go back to **Settings → Accessibility**.
2. Scroll to the bottom. Tap **Accessibility Shortcut**.
3. Check **Color Filters** only (if you check more than one, triple-click opens a menu instead of flipping red instantly).

After that:

- **Triple-click the side button** (Face ID) or the **Home button** (older phones) — red on. Triple-click again — red off.
- Optional: on Display & Text Size, turn on **Reduce White Point** so even the red is dimmer.
- Apple: [Color Filters](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111773) · [Accessibility Shortcut](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/quickly-turn-accessibility-features-on-or-off-iph3e2e31a5/ios).
- Walkthrough with the same path: [Gadget Hacks — hidden red screen](https://ios.gadgethacks.com/how-to/keep-your-night-vision-sharp-with-iphones-hidden-red-screen-0173903/).

## 7. Headlamps, fixtures, and what to buy

We don't sell gear. These are the kinds of products that make kinder lighting easy — red when you need it, warm and shielded when you don't. Look for the spec.

**What to look for (any brand)**

- **A real red mode** — a dedicated red LED, not a white light with a clip-on gel you'll lose.
- **A low setting** — 5–40 lumens for camp and walking. If the lowest mode is 200, it's a floodlight on your forehead.
- **Lockout** — so it doesn't fire white in a bag or a pocket.
- **Aim down** — a flood on the ground, not a spotlight at eye height.
- **For the house / street** — **DarkSky Approved**, **≤2700K** (3000K max), **fully shielded**, dimmable. If you can see the bare bulb from the sidewalk, skip it.

**Headlamps (red + dim built in)**

- **[Petzl TIKKA](https://www.petzl.com/INT/en/Sport/Headlamps/TIKKA)** — the classic: white flood plus a real red LED, light enough that people actually wear it.
- **[Black Diamond Spot](https://www.blackdiamondequipment.com/en_US/product/spot-400-headlamp/)** — red night-vision mode, power tap, and a lock so it stays off in a pack.
- **[Fenix HM50R](https://www.fenixlighting.com/products/fenix-hm50r-v2-0-headlamp)** — compact, USB-C, red side light — a good "always in the glove box" lamp.
- Buying primer (what the lumens on the box actually mean): [REI — How to Choose a Headlamp](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/headlamps.html).

**Home and street fixtures**

- **[DarkSky Approved](https://darksky.org/what-we-do/darksky-approved/)** — third-party seal. If it's certified, it already meets shielding / glare / skyglow rules.
- **[Search approved luminaires](https://darksky.org/what-we-do/darksky-approved/darksky-approved-luminaires-program/luminaires/)** — path lights, porch lights, street heads. Filter, then buy that exact SKU (the unshielded twin is often sitting next to it).
- **[DarkSky Approved retailers](https://darksky.org/what-we-do/darksky-approved/)** — start here if you want a porch or path light this weekend, not a municipal bid.
