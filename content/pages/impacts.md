---
title: Why bad lighting is a terrible idea
visibility: public
---

Light at night isn't free. It has a measurable biological cost — to you, to wildlife, and to the planet. Here's what the science says.

## Your health

Your body runs on a circadian clock tuned to light and dark. Blue-rich light at night — exactly what cool-white LEDs and screens emit — is the strongest signal your brain uses to suppress **melatonin**, the hormone that tells your body it's time to sleep.

- Exposure to light at night is associated with **poor sleep, fatigue, and metabolic disruption**.
- The **American Medical Association** has warned that high-intensity, blue-rich street lighting (4000K+) creates glare that impairs night vision for drivers and can suppress melatonin.
- Epidemiological studies link chronic night-shift work and light-at-night exposure to elevated risks of certain cancers (notably breast and prostate), which is why the World Health Organization classifies shift work that involves circadian disruption as a probable carcinogen.

A street lamp shining into a bedroom window isn't just annoying — it's a low-dose biological stressor, every single night, for years.

## Wildlife

Living things evolved around a dark night. Artificial light scrambles that.

- **Birds** migrating at night are disoriented by bright buildings and towers, circling until exhausted; collisions with lit structures kill hundreds of millions of birds a year.
- **Insects** spiral into lights and die — disrupting pollination and the base of the food web.
- **Sea turtle hatchlings**, which navigate toward the brightest horizon (historically the moonlit ocean), crawl toward inland lights and roads instead.
- **Bats, frogs, and fireflies** retreat from lit areas, fragmenting habitat and silencing ecosystems that depend on them.

## The sky and the climate

Light pollution erases the night sky — the oldest shared human experience — and it does so while burning real energy. The International Dark-Sky Association estimates that in the U.S. alone, roughly **30% of outdoor lighting is wasted**, shining upward or elsewhere it's not needed. That's billions of dollars and millions of tons of CO₂ spent lighting the undersides of clouds.

Every lumen aimed at the sky is money and carbon with no benefit. Fixing it isn't sacrifice — it's the rare environmental win where the *better* choice is also the *cheaper* one.
