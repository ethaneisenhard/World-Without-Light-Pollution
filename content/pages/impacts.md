Warm, aimed, modest light is easier on sleep, wildlife, and the night sky. Here's what the research shows.

## Your health

Your body keeps time with light and dark. **Warm, dim light after dusk** helps melatonin rise — the signal that it's time to rest.

- Sleep comes more easily when evenings stay amber and low, not blue-white and bright.
- The **American Medical Association** notes that high-intensity, cool street lighting (4000K+) is harder on drivers' night vision and on the body's night signals. Warmer, shielded light is easier on both.
- Shift work that keeps people in bright light overnight is linked to long-term health strain. At home, a darker bedroom is one of the gentlest things we can offer.

A street lamp that stays on your street — not in your window — lets a whole block rest.

## Wildlife

Night is habitat. Kind lighting leaves room for it.

- **Birds** migrating after dark follow the stars and the horizon. Softer, downward light helps them stay on course.
- **Insects** pollinate and feed everything above them. Lights that stay on the ground give them a way past.
- **Sea turtle hatchlings** look for the moonlit ocean. Dark beaches and warm, shielded streets help them find the water.
- **Bats, frogs, and fireflies** keep to the unlit edges. A dimmer neighborhood is a wider home.

## The sky we share

- A dark sky is the oldest thing we have in common. Aimed light lets the stars stay.
- DarkSky International estimates that in the U.S. alone, roughly **30% of outdoor lighting** never reaches the ground. Pointing it down is the same as getting that light back — for seeing, and for the sky.
- Warm, modest fixtures cost less to run. The kinder choice is often the quieter bill too.
